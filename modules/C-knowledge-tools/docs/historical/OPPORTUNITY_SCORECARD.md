# Product Opportunity Scorecard

## Method

Each option is scored 1-5. Higher is better, including the safety criterion. Scores are evidence-informed judgments, not measured demand.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 means |
|---|---:|---|
| Pain severity and frequency | 15% | Frequent, consequential problem with an existing workaround |
| Willingness to pay / ACV | 15% | Clear budget owner and plausible high contract value |
| Corpus-derived differentiation | 15% | The source creates a distinctive, useful workflow rather than content decoration |
| Competitive whitespace | 15% | Few strong products foreground the same artifact and job |
| Distribution access | 10% | Reachable users and credible channel partners |
| Retention and data loop | 10% | Repeated use compounds value and switching cost |
| MVP feasibility | 10% | Core value can be proven without regulated integrations or frontier research |
| Safety and regulatory fit | 10% | Low claim risk, clear boundaries, privacy-compatible |

## Rejections

### A. Consumer consciousness score

Fast to build but fundamentally weak. The book's percentage ranges are unvalidated, a global score encourages status comparison, and repeated use has little intrinsic value.

### B. Spiritual knowledge companion

The corpus is differentiated, but public delivery risks redistributing copyrighted expression and presenting speculative claims as fact. The addressable paying niche is unlikely to support the target without unrelated expansion.

### C. Consumer AI journal

Rosebud, Mindsera, Reflection, Day One, and Stoic already cover prompts, AI conversation, emotion/pattern detection, and private journaling at roughly consumer subscription prices. Differentiation and ACV are too low.

### D. BodyOS expansion

The existing deterministic engine is strong, but it is a different product with an active dirty worktree. Fitness/health advice adds safety burden, and the supplied corpus does not create a unique readiness moat.

### E. Human-coach workspace

A credible channel and possible second product surface. Its limitation is fragmented buyers and lower ACV. It becomes the channel/edition for the selected product rather than the company-defining wedge.

### G. Team retrospective intelligence

High value and strong retention, but shared incident data creates politics and monitoring risk before individual trust is established. It is a later expansion after users control their own records.

## Selection

### F. Private manager decision memory

Working name: **Praxis Ledger**.

Job to be done:

> When a consequential decision or difficult conversation is still fresh, help me separate facts from my story, record what I expect, and learn from what actually happened, so I improve my next response without exposing a private diary to my employer.

Core artifact:

`event -> facts -> interpretation -> intended action -> predicted outcome -> actual outcome -> evidence-linked pattern -> if-then cue`

Why it can win:

- The book contributes a deep pattern vocabulary and repeated reflection/practice structure.
- The product rejects the book's unvalidated scores and preserves only observable evidence.
- Existing AI coaches foreground conversation; Praxis Ledger foregrounds a durable decision record with prediction/outcome closure.
- Pattern hypotheses show their supporting events and can be accepted, edited, or rejected.
- Private raw notes and explicit sharing address the trust failure of workplace analytics.
- Value compounds as similar situations recur, creating an outcome-labeled personal decision dataset.

## Expansion Sequence

1. **Individual manager:** private event and outcome loops.
2. **Coach edition:** client shares selected loops; coach assigns/reviews implementation cues.
3. **Leadership program:** organization sees activation and loop-completion metrics, never private text.
4. **Team learning:** opt-in, redacted shared decision records and threshold-aggregated process themes.
5. **Integrations:** calendar/Slack/Teams cues, with no passive content ingestion by default.
